the obvious question
01 · SELECTION

Which method, for this model?

AWQ, GPTQ, and INT8 trade off speed, memory, and quality differently depending on your architecture and hardware. Guess wrong and you lose a week finding out.

02 · VERIFICATION

Is it still accurate?

Compress it carelessly and your model quietly gets worse — often on a metric nobody's watching. That has to be checked before it ships, not assumed.

03 · MAINTENANCE

Is it still true next month?

A script you ran once isn't a system. Every new checkpoint needs the same work redone, by someone who still remembers how they did it last time.

We didn't invent compression. We built the thing that makes it safe to do more than once.
how it works

Same weights. Fewer bits. Verified answers.

Live telemetry

Reads real GPU wattage off the hardware while your model runs and turns it into a live $/hour and estimated gCO₂ figure — no spreadsheet math.

Automated quantization

Tests INT8 and INT4 against your specific model and picks whichever keeps output quality intact — instead of you reading three papers to decide.

Instant rightsizing

Once your model needs less memory, EcoScale tells you the smallest GPU that still fits it — so you stop renting an A100 for a job a T4 can handle.

"Cost" blends two things: lower electricity draw at lower precision, and the ability to move onto a cheaper GPU tier once VRAM demand drops. Accuracy retention varies by model and task — EcoScale runs a verification pass before recommending any switch, rather than assuming compression is free.
who actually needs this

We're not built for the companies that build the models.

Foundation-model labs already employ hundreds of engineers whose entire job is squeezing efficiency out of their own models. They were never going to be a customer — and that's fine.

Not who we're built for

  • Foundation-model labs with dedicated ML-infra teams
  • Teams already running custom CUDA optimization kernels
  • Companies whose entire product is the model

Who actually uses this

  • Startups fine-tuning or self-hosting open models — Llama, Mistral, Qwen
  • Small teams shipping one AI feature inside a larger product
  • Companies renting cloud GPUs who are tired of guessing what size they need
